Swing = how many points better than the line the winner played. Only games with a logged Meech line count, so the list starts Nov 4, 2026.?How home court is measured. For each of the last four full seasons (the COVID year is skipped), every team gets a full-season rating. Then we check how each team did in its home games compared with what its rating said, and the same for its road games. A team’s raw home edge is its home result minus its road result. That raw number is noisy, so it’s pulled toward the league average: a team’s own history counts as n / (n + 200) of its edge, where n is the home games measured. A team with 60 home games keeps about 23% of its own edge; the rest comes from the league average. Altitude teams rise to the top on their own: the model is never told anything about altitude.

The Meech Model rates every Division I men's and women's basketball team and puts out a line for every game before tip-off. Every line gets graded against the final score and against Vegas. The point is twofold:

  1. To see if a model built from scratch can hang with the numbers and ranking systems everybody else uses.
  2. Create a model that uses the same engine to rate both NCAA men's and women's teams in a similar manner in order to produce lines.

Rating every team

Game data must be normalized per 100 possessions. A team that scores 85 in a fast-paced game and a team that scores 65 in a slow-paced grinder can be equally good. Each team gets three numbers, all adjusted for who they played:

Offense

Points scored per 100 possessions above an average D1 offense.

Defense

Points allowed per 100 above an average D1 defense. Lower is better.

Tempo

Possessions per 40 minutes the team plays at against an average opponent.

Net rating = offense − defense. That's the ranking.

Where a team starts

Before anyone plays, each team gets a starting rating built from last year's team rating, its strength of conference, last season's production from returning players and transfers, and recruiting rankings for incoming freshmen. That starting point counts for about six games' worth of evidence for men's teams and four games' worth for women's, then this season's results take over.

Conferences move together

Early in the season, most teams have played only a couple of tough opponents. When a whole conference keeps beating expectations, or keeps getting blown out, every team in it moves slightly. Once conference play starts, that effect will lock in place.

Making the line

  • Matchup: each offense against the other defense, at the pace both teams play.
  • Home court advantage: every team gets its own specific home edge from four seasons of home-vs-road results. Most are 3 to 4 points, but some high altitude teams like Utah, BYU and Colorado are over 5. Neutral sites get none.
  • Buy games: in November and December, a Power-conference team hosting a smaller school gets a small boost, because historic data says those game lines often underestimate the Power 5 team.
  • Win %: how often a favorite of that size actually wins, based on how far real games land from the line. A game with a higher spread, meaning one team is a heavy favorite, will have a higher win % for the favorite.

How every piece earned its spot

There are many inputs that can be placed into models of this kind. In order for an idea to be incorporated into The Meech Model, it was tested using the 2021-22 through 2024-25 seasons as test data. Then, it was graded on the 2025-26 season to remove any training bias. If an idea didn't make the lines more accurate, the idea was not used as an input in the model. One exception: after that check, the buy-game boost was refit using all five seasons, including 2025-26, so this season’s live track record is the true test.

On the 2025-26 grading season the model missed the final margin by about 9.1 points per men's game and 9.7 per women's game, picking the winner 72% and 77% of the time.

Power ratings vs. rankings

Power ratings and rankings answer different questions. Power ratings ask who is strongest right now. These ratings are what the lines come from, and a team can be rated above a team with a better record. Rankings ask who's earned the most, based on who you've played and beaten this season.

In my opinion, teams should not be ranked until after the Thanksgiving tournaments. In early November, nobody has a true résumé, so the first Meech rankings will drop December 1. How they're built will be explained here when they launch.

What's different here than other rating models

  • Transparent, team-specific home court instead of one number for everybody.
  • Conference-aware ratings that adjust fast in November.
  • Women's basketball, rated and graded using the same engine as men's.
  • A public, never-edited track record against Vegas.
  • Pace control: which teams make opponents play their speed and which ones adapt. This project is in progress, and may be a future input into the Meech Model.
